# Stage S5: recording and automation

Built Nov 21, 2026, from [synth.md](synth.md) "Build plan > S5", with the raw input beside the synth that the owner added to this stage ("The raw input beside the synth" in synth.md). Record (beside Capture, and in the Perform view) records a pass: the mic into the selected layer, and the notes, knob and controller moves, wheel, bend, pressure and scene taps played while it runs, all committed as one undo step. Clips carry automation lanes, drawn in a strip under each layer in the timeline. A layer can let its raw input (the mic live, the take on playback) through beside the synth and crossfade between them.

### The offset in detail

`offset_between_the_raw_input_and_the_synth_it_drives` in `crates/klont/tests/s5.rs`: a sung-like tone (220 Hz, five harmonics, vibrato) starts with a 2 ms attack at 0.6 s. A sine voice holds a note from before the onset; a layer route from the sung volume opens its amp (no smoothing in the route; the amp gain value smooths over its 15 ms). The patch has only the input element, so one render at mix 0 is the synth alone and one at mix 1 the raw input alone. The time each reaches half of its steady level (a 1 ms envelope) is compared.

| Path | Raw input at half level | Synth at half level | Synth minus raw | Raw held back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Live: the layer listens to the mic | 8.15 ms | 10.12 ms | +1.98 ms | 5 ms |
| Playback: the take of the same singing | 28.15 ms | 25.92 ms | -2.23 ms | 25 ms |

The synth's level response follows the sung volume, which the live tier knows about 5 ms after the mic hears it, then waits for the next control tick (up to 0.67 ms) and the amp gain's own smoothing; that accounts for the +2 ms live. On playback the voices and layer routes read the take's stored frames, which the frame sink shifts two frames later so level lines up with pitch; the take's audio is held back the same 25 ms. Pitch is later than level live (about 24 ms); the live hold-back follows the level, since holding the voice back 24 ms would be heard as a late monitor.

## Where the build differs from the design

- **Level and pan stay layer fields.** Lanes can move them (`layer.level` in dB, `layer.pan`), but they are not values, so routes and scenes cannot.
- **Lanes hold at their ends.** Before a lane's first point it holds that value, after its last point the last; it does not wrap round the loop the way the morph lane does.
- **Hand moves while recording leave their lane** until the transport stops or recording ends (`MSG_RECORD`). "Moving it by hand while recording automation overwrites the lane from that point on": the recorded points replace the old ones from the first move to the end of the pass. Moves while not recording leave the lane in charge, as designed.
- **One undo step per pass.** During a pass, controller, knob, macro, level and pan moves are not committed one by one (S4 committed a controller after 500 ms of rest); where the hand left them is committed with the pass.
- **Listen during playback.** While the transport plays a take on a listening layer and no pass records, the app holds Listen off for that layer so it reads its take; Listen applies again when the transport stops or a pass records. Otherwise playing back a pass recorded into a listening layer would read the silent mic.
- **A take into a listening layer** keeps the live tier's frames as they were heard: no second pass, so no repaired pitch and no sung notes (its notes come from MIDI or the keys).
- **Play from a stop cuts what rings**: sequenced notes still sounding from the last playback, effect tails and the master DC blocker, besides restarting the random sequences. Notes played live (keys, MIDI, the mic) sound on. Without the cut, a long release from the last playback would sound over the start of the next.
- **Exports settle before they play.** A fresh engine glides every value in from the patch's starting values after the patch and layer load, under the first notes of an export; a live engine that has sat still has them settled. The export now renders one second with the transport stopped before Play, and Play from a stop resets the random state, so the second leaves nothing else behind. The golden renders do not settle: the engine they compare with never did, and settling its reference moves its free-running state.
- **The oscillator's silent skip** advanced its phase by one multiply per span, while playing adds per sample, so a silent oscillator's phase depended on how a tick was split into calls. It now adds per sample. It came from S3 and was found by the sweep check; exports and live play call in multiples of 32 samples, so only other call sizes were affected.
- **The mix point is a channel element.** The design asked for the simplest structure that fits the patch model: the existing ordered chain gains one element, and its place in the chain is the blend. One chain on both is the element first; separate chains are effects before it, each on one path. A raw-path effect counts against the same caps (one reverb, two delays per layer).
- **Take audio in one blob** needed a larger staging area: 16 MB instead of 4 MB, about 12 MB more memory per engine instance (the worklet and the export worker).
- **The strip draws one lane per layer** at a time. Recorded and drawn points are lines (the point that starts a new recorded lane is a step); the strip has no control to change a point's shape to ease or step, though the document and the engine take all three.
- **Where a played note lands** comes from the newest clock reading (taken each animation frame) plus the time since; the note reaches the engine at the next quantum. The check feeds the recorder exact samples; in the page the placement can be off by about a quantum plus the frame's jitter.
- **The checks run through the wasm engine in Node**, with the app's recorder, compiler, store and journal, rather than in a browser with a MIDI device and a mic. The browser run above covered the page with the on-screen keys only.

## Take alignment

Built Nov 21, 2026, answering question 1: read every take's frames earlier so the raw voice, the synth and the beat line up on playback.

**What changed.** The curve pool reads every take's stored frames two frames (20 ms) ahead of the take time it is asked for: the frame sink's shift toward the pitch delay (`align_frames` in `dsp/src/capture.rs`, `take_lag_frames` in `live.rs`, `CurvePool::set_lead` in `curve.rs`). This covers both readers, the layer's sung features and routes (`take_frame` in `engine.rs`) and each sequenced voice's curve (`read_frame` in `layer.rs`). The take's audio is no longer held back (`take_audio` in `engine.rs`). Live behaviour is unchanged: the mic is still held back 5 ms.

**Why 20 ms and not 25.** The design reasoned that the frames lag their audio by the level delay plus the shift, 25 ms. Measured, they lag by about 20.8 ms (the playback synth came 2.23 ms before the raw input when both were 25 ms apart). Reading 25 ms ahead puts the synth 2.27 ms before the voice it follows; reading 20 ms ahead puts it 2.44 ms after, close to the live 1.98 ms. The 20 ms is a whole number of frames, so the reads land on the same frame boundaries as before.

**Measured** with `cargo test -p klont --release --test s5 -- --nocapture` (the same sung tone, onset at 0.6 s):

| Path | Raw input | Synth minus raw input |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live: the layer listens to the mic (held back 5 ms) | 8.15 ms after the sung onset | +1.98 ms |
| Playback: the take, before | 25 ms behind the beat | -2.23 ms |
| Playback: the take, now | on the beat (0 samples; the test allows one 32-sample control tick) | +2.44 ms |

The mix at 1 now nulls against the take's audio at its own time at -159.0 dB. The test asserts the raw input is within one control tick of the beat, playback within 10 ms of live, and both syntheses within 0 to 5 ms after their raw input.

**What else changed.** Two golden renders changed because their takes now play earlier: `sung-melody` and `gestures`, the only fixtures whose reference uploads hold a take curve. The reference engine (from before S1) reads frames at the take time, so their reference curve blobs (`uploads/02-3-0.bin`) were regenerated without their first two frames, which is what that engine reads when it reads 20 ms ahead. The take data the current side compiles (`curves/`) is unchanged. They now match within -132.7 dB and -124.5 dB; every other golden render is unchanged. `beat-take` has a take but no curve upload, so it plays as before. The export and live bit-identity check and the determinism checks pass unchanged, as do the S5 record checks in `record.test.ts`.

**For the owner.** Every existing take with a melody or gesture curve now drives its synth 20 ms earlier than before, and its raw voice plays 25 ms earlier. How the second pass places notes against the frames was not looked at here.

## Live playback against the export, again

Found Nov 22, 2026, while checking Q1: the record check (check 1) gave a worst difference of -171.1 dBFS between live playback and the export on `main`, where this page recorded no sample differing. The check still passed, since it asserted the design's -90 dB.

**Where it came from.** Take alignment (`28a10b2`, in the wasm from `6175ab7`). The record check at `6035a25` and `28a10b2` gives no difference; from `6175ab7` on it gives -171.1 dBFS. Reading frames ahead was not the cause: with the take's curve left out, the difference is the same, and with its audio left out there is none. What changed is that the take's audio no longer starts 25 ms late, so the raw input is heard from the first sample of playback.

**The cause.** The blend's gains (`Ramp` in `layer.rs`) step to their target over each 32-sample control tick and stopped only when they overshot it. Adding the step 32 times can stop an ulp or two short; the next tick's step is then too small to move the gain, so it keeps that remainder until the target moves. The live engine, after recording a pass, and a fresh export engine held the same mix a few ulps apart. The difference (one ulp either way on the raw input) lasted until the lane's first glide had gone through, the first 215 samples. With the take's audio silent for the first 25 ms, nothing passed through the blend while the two gains differed, so it never showed.

**The fix.** The ramp now lands exactly on its target on the last sample of each tick, so a settled gain no longer depends on what it did before. Nothing audible changes: the gains differ from before by a few ulps at most, and only on layers with an input element. The golden renders and every other check pass unchanged, and the engine figures in this page's tables are the same.

**Measured** with `cd web && npx vitest run --silent=false src/record/record.test.ts`: live playback against the export, no sample differs (peak 0.75), and the same with Feel on in Q1's grid section. The check now asserts that no sample differs, so a difference this small fails it. `pnpm test`: 437 Vitest tests in 57 files and 347 Rust tests pass (load 18; `ten_seconds_matches_quickly` in the analysis crate went over its 1 s limit once in an earlier run at load 18 and passed on the rerun).

The layer's level and pan ramps (`gl`, `gr`) are built the same way and can keep a remainder in the same way. They were not changed: no check shows a difference from them, and they run on every layer, so changing them moves every golden render.
