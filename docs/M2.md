# M2: capture

M2 builds the capture pipeline from the [TDD](TDD.md) build order: the live tier inside the M1 engine, the second pass, tuning, and from them notes, hits, hit shapes and kit slots. Its gate is **50 ms follow, hums kept**:

- **50 ms follow:** from voice to ear, Live follow stays within 50 ms on wired audio (PRD target, TDD performance table).
- **Hums kept:** at least 80% of hums give a melody worth keeping (TDD testing table, melody analysis).

This page says how to run it, how to judge the gate on the reference laptop (2020 M1 MacBook Air, Chrome), what is built and what was measured on the build machine.

## Run it

```sh
pnpm install
pnpm dev
```

Open the URL Vite prints. The index page has a new M2 section:

- **`m2.html`, the capture page.** Press Start (the browser asks for the mic), put on wired headphones, pick Melody or Beat and a sound, and sing. Hold Space (or hold the capture button) to record a take; let go and the take is analysed and loops as notes or hits.
- **`delay.html`** now measures the M2 engine by default (add `?engine=m0` for the M0 one) and has a new "Engine follow test (no mic)" button.

The built `klont.wasm` and `analysis.wasm` are committed in `web/public/wasm`, so no Rust toolchain is needed. Tests: `pnpm test` (Vitest, then `cargo test --workspace`).

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `cargo test --release -p klont --test live_follow -- --nocapture` | Engine-side follow delay, native, with numbers |
| `cargo test --release -p analysis --test hum_corpus -- --nocapture` | Hums kept on the synthetic hum corpus, every hum listed |
| `cargo test --release -p analysis --test beat_corpus -- --nocapture` | Beat detection, grouping and tempo on the synthetic beat corpus |
| `cargo run -p analysis --release --bin note_eval -- [--melody] <dir>` | Hums kept on real labelled recordings (see "Scoring real hums") |
| `cargo run --release -p klont --bin bench` | As in M1, now also the live tier cost per block |

## Judge the gate

The two halves are judged differently. The follow delay has an engine part that is measured and a device part that depends on the laptop and the headphones. Hums kept needs a person humming.

### 1. Follow delay, measured (delay page)

On the 2020 M1 MacBook Air, charger in, other apps and tabs closed:

1. Run `pnpm dev`, open `http://localhost:5173/delay.html` in Chrome. It should say `crossOriginIsolated: true` and "Engine for the mic test: M2 klont".
2. Press **Engine follow test (no mic)**. It runs a test hum through the engine's AudioWorklet in an offline audio context: the engine-side delay only, no devices. Expect level about 2 ms and pitch about 24 ms (the build machine's figures).
3. Connect the output you will sing with (wired headphones or an audio interface) and give the mic a path from that output: a loopback cable from output to input, or the headphones held against the mic. Without a path the round trip finds no clicks.
4. Press **Run all**. It measures the device round trip from five chirps, then the engine's delay on a hum played through the speakers and heard by the mic.
5. Read **Voice to ear**: round trip, engine level and pitch, and their totals against 50 ms. Press **Download JSON** and keep the file.

What to expect: the engine adds about 2 ms for level and about 24 ms for pitch. Everything else is the devices. The owner's earlier round trip on built-in speakers and mic was 70.6 ms (M0), already over 50 ms before the engine adds anything, so that setup cannot pass and wired headphones or an interface are needed. A pass needs a round trip of about 25 ms or less for pitch (25 + 24) and about 47 ms or less for level.

### 2. Follow delay, by ear (capture page)

1. Run `pnpm dev`, open `http://localhost:5173/m2.html`, wired headphones on, press **Start** and allow the mic. Check the transport says shared.
2. Choose **Melody** and a sound (Choir pad or Reed are clearest), set Mouth to 100%, Snap to 0.
3. Sing "ta ta ta", then a slow phrase. The synth should feel attached to your voice: note starts with no audible lag, no flam on "ta".
4. Hold **Space**, sing about 10 seconds, let go. The gate panel shows **engine follow: level X ms, pitch Y ms** for that take, measured from your own voice and the engine's output on the same sample clock, plus the device latency Chrome reports.
5. Press **Play voice + synth**: your raw voice in the left ear, the live synth in the right, as recorded. Listen for the offset on note starts. This is the engine part only; add the round trip from step 1.

**Pass (follow):** engine follow on your takes is under about 30 ms for pitch and a few ms for level, the measured total on delay.html is 50 ms or less on the wired setup, and singing feels attached ("none" or "slight" delay, as in the M0 checklist).

### 3. Hums kept (capture page)

1. On `m2.html`, Melody layer. Hum or sing a short phrase (3 to 12 notes, 3 to 10 s) while holding Space.
2. On release the take is analysed (the time is shown; budget 2 s) and loops on the engine. Its notes are drawn as bars over the pitch curve. Root, scale and ref are filled into Tune.
3. Listen to the loop. Press **Keep** if you would keep this melody as it is, **Redo** if a note is missing, extra, split, or on the wrong pitch.
4. Repeat for at least 10 hums, with varied phrases: legato, detached, repeated notes, a slide, some vibrato. Ideally several voices.
5. The tally reads "kept N of M (P%)" against 80%. Press **Download JSON** to keep the scores, settings and numbers.

**Pass (hums kept):** the tally is 80% or more.

Beats are not part of the gate, but are worth a listen: choose **Beat**, beatbox a bar or two, and check the loop. Teach my kit (hold a slot button and repeat one sound) makes live and stored hits go to the slot you taught.

### Scoring real hums

To turn real hums into numbers the way the synthetic corpus is scored: on `m2.html` press **Save take** after a take. It downloads the mic audio as WAV, the frames as `.f32` and the second-pass result as JSON. Put the WAVs in a folder, and next to each `<name>.wav` write `<name>.notes.csv` with one line per sung note: `onset_s,offset_s,pitch` (MIDI note number, or Hz with a header whose third column is `hz`). Then run `cargo run -p analysis --release --bin note_eval -- --melody <folder>`. It prints note precision, recall and F1 per hum and the kept share (a hum is kept when its note F1 is 0.9 or better).

## What was measured on the build machine

Build machine: Apple M4 MacBook Air, Chrome 154 (headless for browser runs), 48 kHz, 128-frame render quantum. No one sang into it: automated Chrome cannot open a microphone, so the hum figures are from the synthetic corpus.

### Follow delay, engine side

| Where | Level delay | Pitch lock (within 50 cents) | How |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Native | 1.75 and 0.29 ms (220 and 330 Hz notes) | 30 and 26 ms | `live_follow` test, synthetic hum, 128-sample blocks |
| klont.wasm in Node | 2.3 ms median (6 bursts, 2.25 to 2.42) | 24 ms median (24 to 28) | `web/src/delay/klont-delay.test.ts`, the delay page's room-modelled hum |
| Chrome AudioWorklet | 2.3 ms median | 24 ms median | delay.html "Engine follow test (no mic)", OfflineAudioContext |
| Nominal (engine meters) | 5.0 ms | 23.5 ms | `M_DELAY_LEVEL`, `M_DELAY_PITCH` |

Level output starts in the same render quantum as the input that opened the gate. The pitch share is set by the YIN window (32 ms at about 16 kHz) and is the same as M0's.

Device delay was not measured in M2 (no one at the build machine). Reported by Chrome on the M4: baseLatency 5.3 ms, outputLatency 40 ms on built-in speakers (M0). Owner's MacBook, built-in speakers and mic: round trip 70.6 ms (M0).

| Totals with the owner's 70.6 ms round trip | Level | Pitch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engine + devices | about 73 ms | about 95 ms |

So with built-in speakers the follow gate fails on the devices alone. The gate needs the round trip measured on wired headphones or an interface.

### Hums kept

Synthetic corpus, 55 hums from 11 voices (low bass to child, hum/oo/aa formants, breath noise, jitter, room rumble, ±25 cent intonation drift), vibrato, scoops, legato and detached phrasing, repeated notes split by dips or "da" consonants, slides; 409 notes. A found note matches a true note when its onset is within 50 ms and its pitch within 50 cents.

| Measure | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Note precision | 0.993 |
| Note recall | 0.978 |
| Note F1 | 0.985 |
| Hums kept (F1 0.9 or better) | 51 of 55 = 92.7% (target 80%) |

The four not kept: a breathy baritone "aa" whose YIN confidence stays near 0.6, so it reads as texture with no notes; a breathy baritone hum missing two quiet notes; a child's hum with two slides missed; a rough bass with two extra notes. Treat 92.7% as an upper bound: the corpus is synthetic, and the real figure is the capture page tally or `note_eval` on labelled recordings.

### How it works

- **Live tier inside `ew_process`.** Before the voices, the M0 live tier runs over the block's mic input (high-passed in place), the tuner runs once per YIN hop with the armed layer's Tune settings, and every finished frame is written as a 25-float record (the 24-float frame plus the tuned pitch, the curve-table layout). `ew_render` (offline) does not run it.
- **One code path for live and stored.** An armed melody or texture layer gets a live voice whose controls come from a frame record built from the live values each control tick, through the same `controls()` and `gesture_offsets()` that stored takes use. A voice starts when the gate opens and restarts on each onset, with the onset's hardness; it is released when the gate closes and scaled by the gate envelope so it fades without clicks.
- **Live hits.** An armed beat layer fires a hit per onset. The slot is the nearest taught centre in live-hit-feature space, or, with nothing taught, brightness thirds into slots 0 to 2 over a range that adapts to recent hits (it starts at 0.3 to 0.7, so the first hits of a session may land one slot off).
- **No bleed check, no automatic mute.** Nothing listens for the engine in the mic and nothing mutes the live sound. There is also no manual feedback control: none was needed with headphones, and the page says to use wired headphones. Speakers in the room will feed back.
- **Takes.** Frame n covers mic samples `[n L, (n+1) L)` with `L` = sample rate / 100, counted from engine start, so a take is a run of frames and the matching mic samples. The page reads both from the rings when Space is released.
- **Second pass.** Runs in a worker on the take's mic audio and frames. Pitch: pYIN repairs the live curve where they differ by more than 80 cents for 30 ms, and fills frames the live tier left unvoiced when pYIN holds a pitch for 60 ms at an audible level. Frames far below the take's level lose their pitch, because the live tier holds the last pitch through its gate release. Timbre is never recomputed. Onsets come from a spectral-flux detector on the audio, not from the live tier's onset slot, which misses re-attacks.
- **Kind detection** uses voiced fraction, onsets per second, median event length, pitch stability, and one feature the TDD does not list: the share of percussive events. Without it a pitched "boom" looks like a hummed note. Thresholds are in `second::thresh`. The capture page passes the kind of the layer you chose, so detection only matters for the reported value.
- **Hit units.** Attack and hold in seconds, bend in semitones per second, endStop and endClick 0 or 1. The TDD does not pin down endStop, endClick or strength; the rules chosen are in `beats.rs`.
- **Playback.** A take becomes one free-time section of the loop length (whole beats when a tempo was found, else the take), looping, with one clip of notes (reading the take's curve) or hits. Changing a Tune setting reruns tuning on the stored take and reloads only the curve.

### What came from M0 and M1

- **Moved:** the live tier (`dsp::capture`) and tuner (`dsp::tune`) now run inside the M1 engine. The pYIN tracker and its repair rule are the second pass's first step.
- **Kept as is:** the M0 engine (`crates/engine`, `engine.wasm`) and every M0 page still run. `engine.wasm` was rebuilt because the shared DSP crate changed. The M1 player and benchmark are unchanged; the host only creates the mic and frame rings when asked for input.
- **Changed:** `delay.html`'s engine test measures the M2 engine unless `?engine=m0` is given. For the M2 engine it reads pitch lock at a clarity of 0.8 instead of 0.95: the M2 output is on pitch from about 25 ms but only reaches 0.95 clarity at about 60 ms.

## Not in M2, on purpose

- **The track and editing** (project document, commands, undo, sections, overdub alignment against the transport): M3. A take plays as its own loop.
- **Round-trip compensation of takes.** Takes are aligned to the engine's sample clock, not to a playing transport, so the round trip does not enter yet. It matters once takes are placed on a track in M3.
- **Storage of takes** (origin private file system): later. Save take downloads files instead.
- **Live Level out.** Level out applies to stored takes only.
- **Influence, grain sources, "use my sound for a kit slot":** M4.

## Known limits

- **Synthetic corpora only.** No real hums have been scored. Breathy voices with weak periodicity are the weak spot in the synthetic set.
- **Fast voiced beatboxing reads as melody.** Pop, boom and nnt patterns at 128 bpm and above look like staccato hummed notes to kind detection. The capture page avoids this by passing the layer's kind.
- **Hats at fast tempos** are sometimes missed, and sparse patterns can settle on half or double tempo.
- **Follow-delay estimate per take** on the capture page uses a pitch window of about two periods, so on low voices it reads a few ms late. The fallback transport can misplace take boundaries by up to 100 ms.
- **Live and stored tuned pitch differ slightly.** Live tuning steps once per 5 ms YIN hop; stored tuning steps per 10 ms frame. The frames themselves match exactly (tested).

## Open questions this milestone touches
