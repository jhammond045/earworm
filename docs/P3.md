# Stage P3: poly takes

Built Nov 22, 2026, from [poly.md](poly.md) "Build plan > P3". A melody or texture layer can now take Poly: play a chord, a riff or a piano part into it, and when the take is committed the analysis worker reads it with Basic Pitch's note model (Spotify, Apache 2.0), ported to Rust. The notes go into the clip, several at once, and each keeps a curve of its own (pitch offset, volume, intensity, hardness, onset, 100 records a second), which the voice playing it reads. An instrument (guitar, piano or other) sets which notes a take keeps, and up to 8 notes sound at once, never more than the patch's voices. Live poly sound (P1), the per-note feature checks (P2) and the channel strip (P4) are not in this stage: a Poly layer is silent while you play, and its take comes back as notes a few seconds after you stop.

## What was built

| Part | Where |
| --- | --- |
| The note model: Basic Pitch's network (a constant-Q transform of 309 bins from 27.5 Hz, harmonic stacking, six small convolutions), from the weights in the `basic-pitch` package's ONNX file, written out by `tools/basic-pitch/export.py` (142 KB). Every product and sum is f32 in a fixed order, so a build gives the same numbers every time; the hot loops use wasm SIMD in the browser. | `crates/analysis/src/poly/model.rs`, `basic_pitch.bin` |
| Basic Pitch's note decoding (`note_creation.py` with `predict`'s defaults: onset 0.5, frame 0.3, notes over 11 frames, inferred onsets, the "melodia trick"), step for step, float64 where the Python is. The melodia step visits cells in one sorted order instead of rescanning the matrix for each note, which gives the same notes. | `poly/decode.rs` |
| The poly pass: the take streams in, is resampled to 22050 Hz (Kaiser-windowed sinc) and run in two-second windows as Basic Pitch cuts them, so a long take is never held whole; the output arrays are made at their final size up front. Then the notes are decoded, kept to the instrument's range and to the layer's channels, and each gets its curve. | `poly/mod.rs` `PolyStream`, `an_poly_begin` / `an_poly_feed` / `an_poly_end` in `lib.rs` |
| Instruments: guitar keeps E2 to E6 with 6 channels by default, piano A0 to C8 with 8, other A1 to C7 with 8 (poly.md "Instruments"). Channels: at most N notes sound together; a new note takes the free channel nearest in pitch whose note ended within a second, else the one free longest, and with every channel busy it replaces the quietest sounding note only if it is 6 dB louder (that note ends there), else it is dropped. | `Options`, `cap_channels` in `poly/mod.rs`; `record/poly.ts` |
| Note curves: per note, 100 records a second over its life. Pitch: the offset in cents from the note's semitone, from the model's pitch contour (the strongest bin within 25 of the note's, Gaussian-weighted as Basic Pitch's pitch bends are, refined by a parabola). Volume: the power of partials 1 to 8 in the take's constant-Q spectrum, 0 at 48 dB below the take's loudest note, 1 at it. Intensity: partials 5 to 10 against 1 to 4, against the take's usual tilt, ±10 dB as 0..1. Hardness: how fast the note's level rose into its start, held. Onset: 1 in the first record. The note's velocity is its volume at the start. | `curves`, `pitch_offset` in `poly/mod.rs` |
| Engine: a note-curve pool beside the take curves, loaded with `LOAD_NOTE_CURVES` (kind 7, one slot per poly take). Track blob v4 ("EWT4") lets a note name its note-curve slot and index in event words 14 and 15; v1 to v3 blobs load as before, and the encoder still writes the oldest version that holds the track. A voice playing such a note reads the take's curve for the whole input's features (openness, breath, the vowel bands) and its note's record over them: pitch is the note's own pitch plus the offset, so a note moved by hand takes its bend with it. | `crates/klont/src/notecurve.rs`, `seq.rs`, `layer.rs` `read_frame`, `voice.rs` `note_intensity` |
| Schema 6: optional `Layer.capture` (mode, instrument, channels), `Note.curve` (index into the clip's take's note curves) and `Take.noteCurves` (a blob, `notecurves-<take id>`). Documents without them play as before; the 5 to 6 step only changes the number. Project zips, the session and cleanup keep the blob. | `project/types.ts`, `migrate.ts`, `commands.ts` `setLayer`, `m3/session.ts`, `project/archive.ts`, `cleanup.ts` |
| Capture: a take into a Poly layer does not arm Live follow while it records; when it ends, the main thread feeds the take to the analysis worker a second at a time (each piece answered before the next goes, so the worker never holds the whole take), then places the notes and stores the curves with the take in one undo step. | `app/capture.ts` `placePoly`, `m2/analysis.ts` `Analysis.polyPass`, `m2/analysis-worker.ts` |
| Compile and export: the compiler sends each played poly take's curves as `LOAD_NOTE_CURVES` and marks its notes in the track; the export carries them too. | `project/compile.ts`, `m1/track.ts` `encodeNoteCurves`, `app/mix.ts`, `m1/render-core.ts` |
| The Perform view: a "Takes" row on melody and texture layers: Mono (one line) or Poly (chords), and for Poly the instrument and "Notes at once" (1 to 8, the counts above the patch's voices greyed out), with a line saying what to expect. Changing the instrument moves the count to its default unless it had been changed. | `app/perform.ts` `captureRow` |
| Checks: `poly_eval` runs the port over labelled recordings and compares it with the Python package; `tools/basic-pitch/reference.py` makes the Python reference with ONNX Runtime; `record/poly.test.ts` is the record check; `poly-bench.html` times the pass in the worker. The license and notice are in `crates/analysis/src/poly/basic_pitch.LICENSE` and `web/public/basic-pitch.LICENSE`, beside `coi-serviceworker.LICENSE`. | as named |

### The port against Basic Pitch in detail

The reference is Basic Pitch 0.4.0's own Python code (`predict`, default settings) with its ONNX model on ONNX Runtime, float32 on the CPU. P0's figures came from the same package through Core ML; they agree with this reference to the third decimal everywhere.

| Stage | Difference from the reference |
| --- | --- |
| The network alone, same 22050 Hz audio (one 22 s guitar excerpt) | Largest difference 3.4e-5 in the note activations, 4.7e-5 in the onsets (0 to 1 each) |
| The decoding alone, on the reference's own outputs | The same 150 notes, start and end times equal to 10 µs |
| End to end | The 0.03 to 0.14% of notes that differ come from the resampler: librosa resamples with soxr, the port with its own windowed sinc, so the model's input differs by a little, and a few activations cross a threshold on the other side. The F1 figures do not move. |

On one 22 s guitar excerpt the curves look as they should: the pitch offset is 1.5 cents at the median (6 cents at p90), volume falls from start to end on 54 of the 71 notes longer than 0.3 s, intensity spreads over 0.07 to 0.91 (p10 to p90) around 0.5, hardness 0 to 0.35. Whether intensity and hardness mean what they should is P2's to test.

## Where the build differs from the design

- **"Arm gains a mode" is a "Takes" setting.** P3 has no live poly sound, so the Arm button still arms mono Live follow; Mono or Poly is a layer setting beside it that decides how a take is read. While a take into a Poly layer records, Live follow is not armed and the layer is silent (poly.md allowed either). P1 can move the setting onto Arm when live poly exists.
- **The instrument sets the range of a take, and the channels apply to takes.** poly.md has the instrument choose the live detector's templates and says the learned model needs no profile. Here the instrument keeps a take's notes to its range (Basic Pitch's own frequency limits) and gives the default channel count, and the channel count caps the notes a take keeps at once, by poly.md's allocation rules, so a take never asks for more voices than the layer plays. On the P0 recordings this changes F1 by 0.001 at most.
- **A note curve's pitch is an offset from the note.** poly.md says each record holds the pitch. Storing the offset means a note moved up or down by hand keeps its bend, and the layer's Tune octave, which the compiler adds to the note, applies to the curve too.
- **Intensity has no template to compare with.** poly.md subtracts the tilt a note's template expects; the note model has no templates, so intensity is the tilt against the take's median tilt. Volume runs over the 48 dB below the take's loudest note.
- **Poly takes skip the mono second pass.** No pYIN repair, tempo or scale detection, and no flex anchors: a flexed clip of a poly take quantizes its note starts (as takes from before Q1 do), and the layer's Tune is left as it was.
- **The pitch centre.** Basic Pitch's own pitch bends measure from a semitone's first contour bin, but the model centres a semitone on its second, so its bends, and the first version of these curves, read a third of a semitone sharp. The curves now measure from the second bin (6.3 cents median error on GuitarSet instead of about 35).
- **The five-minute figure is the worker alone.** The app still stops a take at 59 s (`MAX_TAKE_SEC`), as before.
- **Memory is the worker's wasm memory.** Chrome's `measureUserAgentSpecificMemory` did not attribute the worker's wasm memory, so the page reports the module's memory size, which only grows and so is the peak; the worker holds one second of audio besides.

## Questions for the owner

1. **Takes or Arm.** Keep Mono and Poly as a "Takes" setting until P1, or put it on the Arm button now, with Arm in Poly meaning "record poly takes, no live sound"?
2. **Re-struck notes.** Recall on re-struck guitar notes is 0.703, just over the bar, and it is the model's own figure: the port cannot raise it. Is that enough for strummed rhythm parts, or should P2 or a later stage look at restarting notes from the onsets (Basic Pitch's onset threshold, or the live tier's re-pluck rule) on takes?
3. **Take length.** The pass handles five minutes in the worker, but the app stops a take at 59 s. Raise the limit for poly takes?
4. **Your own recordings.** P0's question 2 still stands: ten minutes of your own playing with a list of what was played is the only check on your guitar, piano and room. `poly_eval run` scores it as soon as it exists.
