# M3: track and editing

M3 builds the track from the [TDD](TDD.md) build order: sections, layers and tempo, undo, ribbons and hit lanes, autosave, and overdub capture placed on the track with the round trip taken out. Its gate is **edit while playing, no loss**:

- **Edit while playing:** any edit (notes, hits, sections, tempo, layers, sounds, undo, redo) reaches the engine while the track plays, without stopping it, without clicks and without the playhead jumping somewhere the edit does not explain.
- **No loss:** the project is saved as you work. A reload loses nothing; a crash or a killed tab loses at most the last 300 ms of edits (TDD "Persistence": each change is journaled within 300 ms).

This page says how to run it, how to judge the gate on the reference laptop (2020 M1 MacBook Air, Chrome), what is built and what was measured on the build machine.

## Run it

```sh
pnpm install
pnpm dev
```

Open the URL Vite prints. The index page has a new M3 section linking **`m3.html`, the track page**:

- **Start (audio and mic)** or **Start without mic** (playback and editing only; **Enable mic** later). The project you had open last is reopened; the first visit makes an "Untitled" project with one 8-beat section at 100 bpm, a Melody layer and a Beat layer.
- **Project:** name, New, Open, Delete, the save status ("Saved" or "Saving N") and storage use (a warning at 80% of the browser's quota).
- **Transport:** Play, Stop, Go to section start, Undo and Redo (also Cmd/Ctrl+Z, Shift+Cmd/Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y).
- **Sections:** click one to select it (while playing this queues a jump to it at the end of the current pass); add, duplicate, remove, move; name, feel (grid or free), beats or seconds, bpm, repeats, tempo ramp.
- **Layers:** up to 8. Kind and sound preset, level, pan, mute, follow (beat layers), mouth and loud depth, and the Tune settings (snap, speed, slides, vibrato, octave, root, scale). Sliders are heard while they move and become one undo step on release.
- **Timeline:** the selected section, one lane per layer. Melody and texture lanes show the take's ribbon (tuned pitch, width from loudness) and note bars; beat lanes show four slot rows of hits. Click to select, Shift+click to add, drag to move (notes also in pitch, snapped to semitones unless Alt is held), drag a note's right edge to resize, drag a hit up or down to change slot, Delete removes, double-click adds a note or a hit. Snap, Zoom, Loop length and Quantize act on the selected clip. Arrow keys, Shift+arrows, comma, period and A edit from the keyboard.
- **Capture (overdub):** pick the layer to capture into, then hold Space (or the button) while the track plays and sing or beatbox along. On release the take goes through the M2 second pass and is placed on the clip where you sang it. If the transport was stopped, holding starts it from the selected section.
- **Edit while playing, no loss:** the gate checks below.

Tests: `pnpm test` (Vitest, then `cargo test --workspace`). The M3 gate test alone, with its numbers printed:

```sh
pnpm vitest run --root web src/project/gate.test.ts --reporter=verbose --silent=false
```

Set `GATE_OUT=<file.json>` to also write the numbers as JSON. The overdub alignment test: `pnpm vitest run --root web src/project/align.test.ts --reporter=verbose --silent=false`.

## Judge the gate

Wired headphones on. There is no feedback protection: nothing checks or mutes the mic, so a speaker in the room will feed back.

### 1. Edit while playing (the page's stress test)

On the 2020 M1 MacBook Air, charger in, other apps and tabs closed:

1. Run `pnpm dev`, open `http://localhost:5173/m3.html` in Chrome, press **Start without mic**. The status should say "Shared transport".
2. Under "Edit while playing, no loss" press **Edit stress test**. It switches to a scratch project called "M3 gate test" (your own project is untouched and reopens next time), plays it, and makes 300 seeded random edits at 10 a second: moving, adding and deleting notes and hits, slot changes, level, pan and mute, bpm, repeats and section length, adding and removing sections, quantize, undo and redo.
3. Listen while it runs. It should keep playing the whole time, with no clicks, gaps or stutters when edits land. Sections may change under you when the test removes the one playing; that is the test, not a fault.
4. Read the result: edits applied, load errors (expect 0), edit-to-engine time (median and max), the highest CPU load, overloads, and whether playback ever stopped. Press **Download JSON** to keep it.

### 2. Edit while playing, by hand

1. In your own project, record a melody on the Melody layer and a beat on the Beat layer (see step 4 below), or double-click to draw a few notes and hits.
2. Press Play. While it plays: drag notes around, change a note's pitch, delete hits, drag the bpm field, add a section and move it before the one playing, change the sound preset, undo and redo several times.
3. **Pass (edit while playing):** the track never stops or stutters, each edit is heard within a beat, nothing clicks, and the section you were hearing keeps playing when you add, remove or move other sections.

### 3. No loss

1. **Reload check:** press it. It saves, reloads the page, reopens the project and shows PASS if the document and the undo history are the same as before the reload.
2. **Crash check:** press it. It makes 20 edits 50 ms apart on a scratch project and reloads straight away without saving. After the reload it shows how many survived and how old the newest lost edit was. Expect a loss of edits from the last 300 ms only.
3. By hand: make a few edits, close the tab (or quit Chrome) within a second, reopen `m3.html`. The edits from before the last third of a second are there, and Undo still steps back through them.
4. **Pass (no loss):** both checks PASS and nothing older than about 300 ms is ever missing.

### 4. Overdub placement, by ear

1. Press **Start (audio and mic)**. Give the mic a path from the output (a loopback cable, or the headphones held against the mic) and press **Measure round trip**. The figure is saved for this input and output pair. Without measuring, the page uses an estimate from Chrome's reported latencies (labelled "estimate").
2. Remove the path, put the headphones on. Draw a beat on the Beat layer (or capture one) and press Play.
3. Pick the Melody layer, hold Space and sing short notes exactly on the beat for a bar or two, then let go.
4. **Pass:** the notes land on the beats in the timeline and play back in time with the beat. Try setting Round trip to 0 and capturing again: the notes should land late by about the round trip.

Build machine: Apple M4 MacBook Air, Node 24 for the engine tests (klont.wasm), Chrome 154 headless for the page, 48 kHz, 128-frame render quantum. No one sang into it: automated Chrome cannot open a microphone, so the alignment figures come from a synthetic singer, and capture on the page was not run.

### Overdub alignment

`src/project/align.test.ts`: a click track at 120 bpm plays through klont.wasm; a synthetic singer sings five notes on the beats as heard, arriving at the mic one round trip (27.3 ms, deliberately not a multiple of the 10 ms frame) later; the take goes through the live tier, the second pass and `placeTake`.

| Round trip used in placement | Note start error against the beat |
| --- | --- |
| 27.3 ms (compensated) | -0.3 ms on each of 5 notes |

A real voice will add onset spread of several milliseconds; the synthetic singer's onsets are clean.

## What is built

| Piece | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Project document (TDD "Data model", with a `takes` map and a `loop` flag) | `web/src/project/types.ts` |
| Patches with inverses and structural sharing | `web/src/project/patch.ts` |
| Commands (23: project, sections, layers, clips, notes, hits, quantize, takes) and `newProject` | `web/src/project/commands.ts` |
| Undo stack of patch groups, serializable; the store | `web/src/project/history.ts`, `store.ts` |
| Autosave: journal in IndexedDB within 300 ms, snapshot every 50 groups, crash recovery; take blobs in the origin private file system through a storage worker | `web/src/project/persist.ts`, `storage-worker.ts` |
| Compiler from document to engine blobs, diffed by object identity; curve slot allocation | `web/src/project/compile.ts` |
| Overdub placement with round-trip compensation | `web/src/project/overdub.ts` |
| Engine: sample clock and transport rate in the meter block (`M_CLOCK_LO`, `M_CLOCK_HI`, `M_UNITS_PER_SAMPLE`); a stable section uid in the track blob | `crates/klont/src/{protocol,engine,seq}.rs`, `web/src/m1/{protocol,track}.ts` |
| Track page: sections, layers, timeline with ribbons and hit lanes, editing, capture, round trip, gate checks | `web/m3.html`, `web/src/m3/*` |
| Gate and alignment tests | `web/src/project/{gate,align}.test.ts`, `web/src/m3/*.test.ts` |

### How it works

- **One immutable document.** Every change is a named command that returns patch ops; the store applies them with structural sharing and keeps the inverse. A drag is one group: while you drag, the page compiles a document with the move applied and sends only the track to the engine; on release it commits one command. Slider drags go straight to the engine as control messages and commit on release.
- **Undo survives a reload.** The undo and redo stacks are part of the snapshot, and the journal records do, undo and redo entries, so replaying it rebuilds both.
- **Compiler.** After each change the compiler re-encodes only what changed: a layer blob when that `Layer` object changed, the track blob when sections, layer order or octaves changed, a curve when its tuned frames changed. Removed layers get a muted layer blob. Tuned curves are computed in the analysis worker when a take or a Tune setting changes and arrive asynchronously.
- **Edits while playing.** The engine already kept its position when a new track blob arrived (M1). M3 adds a stable uid per section, so when sections before the playhead are added, removed or moved, the engine keeps playing the same section at the same position instead of whatever now sits at the old index. Notes already sounding finish their length.
- **Takes.** A take's mic audio and its frames (slot 0 holding the second pass's repaired pitch) are written as `audio-<take>` and `frames-<take>` under `projects/<id>/` in the origin private file system. They are written once the take is released, not while recording.
- **Overdub alignment** (TDD "Take alignment"). When capture starts, the page reads the engine's sample clock and transport position from the meter block. A mic sample at engine sample s is placed at the transport position of s minus the round trip, because the singer sang along to sound that left the engine one output latency earlier and reached the mic one input latency after that. Notes and hits go into the clip at that time, wrapped into the clip's loop, and warp anchors at every note start and end tie the take's curve to the clip.
- **No automatic mute.** Nothing in M3 listens for the engine in the mic or mutes anything. The mic is on only after you press Start or Enable mic.

### What came from M0, M1 and M2

- **Kept as is:** every M0, M1 and M2 page. `m2.html` still captures single takes that loop on their own, without a track.
- **Changed:** the track blob's section record carries a uid in a word that was zero (old blobs still load: uid 0 means none). The meter block has three new slots in a gap. `klont.wasm` was rebuilt; `engine.wasm` and `analysis.wasm` were rebuilt with no change.

## Not in M3, on purpose

- **Influence, grain sources, "use my sound for a kit slot", states and the morph lane:** M4.
- **Take stack browsing.** Every take a clip ever had is kept in the document and on disk, but the page has no control to go back to an older one yet (the `selectTake` command exists).
- **Writing takes while recording.** The TDD has the storage worker drain the mic ring to disk during capture, so a crash mid-take loses at most a second. M3 writes the take on release; a crash during a take loses that take.
- **Project export and import as a zip, schema migrations beyond version 1, deleting orphaned takes:** M5.
- **WebGL2 timeline.** The timeline is Canvas 2D with per-clip geometry cached until the clip or zoom changes. It has not been profiled against the 60 fps budget on the reference laptop.
- **Teach my kit on the track page.** Beat captures use the second pass's own grouping; teaching stays on `m2.html`.
- **A control for the track's loop flag.** New projects loop.

## Known limits

- **A take that runs into the next section** is wrapped into the section where capture started.
- **Capture does not fill Tune** (root, scale, reference) from the second pass, as `m2.html` does. Set them on the layer.
- **The round-trip estimate** from Chrome's figures misses the input side. Measure it once per headphone and interface setup.
- **Notes sounding when their clip is edited** keep sounding for their old length; the edit applies from the next note on.
- **Section uids** are a 32-bit hash of the section id; a collision would only make the engine pick the other section of the pair after a reload.
