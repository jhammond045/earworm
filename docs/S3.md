# Stage S3: editing patches

Built Nov 21, 2026, from [synth.md](synth.md) "Build plan > S3". A layer's patch is no longer fixed to the classic one: the Patch view adds, removes, rewires and reorders modules, envelopes, LFOs and channel effects, shows what the patch costs and how many voices that leaves, and the Preset menu loads, saves, exports and imports presets. Four new modules join the catalog: an oscillator, noise, a sampler and a channel filter.

### Check 2 in detail

A saw from the oscillator alone, one second at 48 kHz, a 4-term Blackman-Harris window; every harmonic up to ten times the sample rate folded back, and the strongest of those against the fundamental. A folded harmonic that lands within 6 Hz of a true harmonic or DC is skipped, because a spectrum cannot tell the two apart. That is why the notes are B's rather than round frequencies: at exactly 2 kHz every folded harmonic lands on a true one (48 kHz is a multiple of 2 kHz). The model below predicts -41.1 dB at 2000 Hz.

| Note | Hz | Strongest alias | At |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G2 | 98.0 | -63.5 dB | 23990 Hz |
| B3 | 246.9 | -55.8 dB | 23800 Hz |
| B4 | 493.9 | -49.8 dB | 23800 Hz |
| B5 | 987.8 | -44.7 dB | 23306 Hz |
| B6 | 1975.5 | -40.4 dB | 22318 Hz |
| B7 | 3951.1 | -38.1 dB | 20343 Hz |
| B8 | 7902.1 | -40.2 dB | 16391 Hz |

The worst alias is always a harmonic just above Nyquist folding to just below it. The usual two-sample polyBLEP gave -31.4 dB at B6, exactly what its kernel (a triangle, response sinc squared) predicts for the 13th harmonic; the four-sample one (the cubic B-spline, sinc to the fourth) predicts -40.6 dB, and measures -40.4. The cost is in the top octave: about 2.5 dB down at 10 kHz and 10 dB at 20 kHz. Above 12 kHz the oscillator falls back to the two-sample version.

### Check 4 in detail

The `worst allowed` scene puts the heaviest patch the caps allow on all 8 layers: 16 voice modules (the partial bank, 4 oscillators, one frequency-modulating another, 2 noises, a sampler, a click, clang, fold, comb, 2 filters, 2 amps, all on), 4 envelopes, 4 LFOs, 48 routes moving the partial bank's spectrum every tick, shaped noise and grains, and a full channel (crush, tape, 3 filters, 2 delays, reverb). Its cost allows 6 voices a layer.

| Part | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| A layer's budget (the classic kit, 8 voices) | 767 ns a sample |
| Classic melody | 73.6 a voice, 123.0 a layer: 8 voices |
| Classic kit | 73.6 a voice, 178.5 a layer: 8 voices |
| Heaviest patch | 101.8 a voice, 140.5 a layer: 6 voices, 751 in all |

The model is close to the measurement: 751 ns a layer predicts 3.47x for 8 layers, against 3.49x and 3.51x measured. So the shortfall comes from the budget, not the meter: the classic structure itself, with fold, clang, comb, grains and its whole channel switched on, costs about 3.4x on this machine. The M1 worst-case scene leaves those off, which is why it renders at 9.6x. For 4x here a layer could spend at most 651 ns; at that budget the classic kit would get 6 voices and the classic melody 7, which would change existing projects.

## Where the build differs from the design

- **The channel filter's type is `cfilter`**, because the voice filter is already `filter`. The interface calls both "filter".
- **No separate Channel view.** The channel's cards (bypass, earlier, later, Remove, and "+ channel effect") sit in the Patch view after the voice's.
- **Crush and tape stay before the first delay or reverb.** They run on the mono voice sum before level and pan, as the S1 note says; the filter may sit anywhere and runs mono or stereo depending on where. A new crush or tape goes before the stereo part; moving one after it is refused in plain words.
- **A sampler plays the layer's sample**, which is its Influence source's steady region (a file, a recording or a saved source), the same sample the grains read. There is no per-module sample, and a sampler is silent until the layer has a source; its card says so. The compiler now sends the sample when the patch has a sampler, not only when "Use the sound itself" is on.
- **Layer sources keep their place.** Shaped noise and grains cannot be added or removed in S3.
- **The fade covers the whole layer**, voices and channel. Delay and reverb tails do not carry over a structure change: they fade with the rest and start empty. Notes that start during the fade wait for the new patch, so they start up to 5.3 ms late. A note that starts while every voice is busy fading is queued, not stolen.
- **Bypass is not structure.** Switching it keeps notes sounding, but bypassing a delay or reverb drops its tail at once, without a fade.
- **Cost counts every module the patch holds, on or off.** A fold whose amount is 0 costs as much as one in use, because the voice count belongs to the structure and must not change with a morph. S2's third question (whether routes that make the partial bank rebuild every tick count): they do, as one surcharge of 11 ns a voice per voice patch when any voice or layer route moves a partial-bank value other than its level, however many routes do.
- **The budget is the classic kit's**, 8 voices plus its four noise banks, grains and channel, so both classic patches keep 8 voices. synth.md said "what 8 classic voices cost"; it now says this.
- **The voicing swap and the silent-frame gate reach the new sources**: oscillators and the sampler fade like the partial bank on unvoiced frames, noise modules rise to the loudest pitched level, and all three are muted on silent frames.
- **The Preset menu builds the factory presets from `m2/sounds.ts`** at start, as the S1 preset files were built; `presets.test.ts` checks the files and the built presets are the same. User presets live under `presets` in the settings store.
- **Check 2 is measured at B6 (1975.5 Hz)**, the note nearest 2 kHz, for the reason under "Check 2 in detail".

## Questions for the owner

1. Check 4 is not met on the build machine: the heaviest allowed patch renders at 3.5x, because the budget is what the classic kit costs with everything switched on, and that alone is about 3.4x here. Options: keep this budget (every existing project keeps 8 voices; a heavy patch can fall below 4x); lower it to about 650 ns so this machine makes 4x (the classic kit would play 6 voices and the classic melody 7, changing old projects); or count fold, clang, comb and the like only while switched on (the voice count would then move with values and morphs). Which do you want, after your run on the reference laptop?
2. The oscillator clears check 2 by 0.4 dB at B6 and misses 40 dB at B7 (-38.1). More margin means running the oscillator at twice the rate with a decimating filter, roughly doubling its 2.2 ns a voice. Is the current figure enough?
3. A structure change empties the delay and reverb (after the 5 ms fade). Should an edit that leaves those effects in place keep their tails? It needs the old and new chains to share instances across the swap.
4. Should a sampler get a sample of its own (an imported file per module) in a later stage, rather than the layer's Influence source?
5. Adding and removing shaped noise and grains was left out of S3. Wanted?
